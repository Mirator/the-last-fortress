# Headless strategy playtest B

Run on 2026-10-15 from source Core at HEAD `cea7c4f`, with .NET SDK 10.0.300. This was an offline simulation requested after the user asked to avoid using their computer. No desktop, Unity Editor, standalone game UI, user save directory, production code, or tests were touched. The driver at `plans/evidence/006-playtest-b/harness/PolicyRunner.csproj` compiles the actual `Runtime/Core/*.cs` files and runs the two existing legal purchase/perk-choice sequences from `MvpPolicyTests`; logs are in `policy-runs.txt`.

The source profile was checked against `Data/MvpDefinitions.asset`: starting gold 250; Keep 500 HP and +20/day; Wall 20 gold/100 HP; Tower 70/100 HP/10 damage/0.75 s/range 4; House 40/80 HP/+15/day; Farm 60/100 HP/+25/day; Trap 35 gold/8 entry damage. All four enemy HP, speed, attack, and wall-multiplier values also match. Wave definitions are generated by `MvpCampaign.SixNightTable()` in Core, with the six entries and enemy counts matching the current authored spec; the asset contains prefab references rather than a separate wave table.

## Findings and next improvements

1. **The economic opening is substantially more forgiving than the fixed defense opening.** The former finishes all three seeds with 500–650 Keep HP and also wins its no-perk seed-1 run. The latter survives only two of three seeded maps with perks, ends those wins at 150 HP, and loses its no-perk seed-1 run. This repeats the prior plan 005 signal under generated terrain. Treat it as a limited balance signal, not proof that players have no viable defensive approach: the defense policy is one fixed action order. Before changing costs or wave tuning, compare one more player-like defensive layout on the same seeds and retain the purchase/reward log.

2. **Generated terrain can invalidate a memorized opening cell.** On economy seed 3, Farm `(4,13)` was rejected as `ImpassableTerrain`; the policy instead bought the House, then spent Day 2 on Farm `(4,11)` and House `(6,12)`, leaving no Tower purchase that day. The run still won. The simulation confirms the rejection reason, but cannot establish whether the ghost and terrain make that reason easy to understand. A follow-up UI session should check whether a player can recover by moving the Farm ghost to open land without losing the economic plan.

3. **The Night 6 combat span ranges from 54.0–59.1 simulated seconds for economy victories and is 67.9 seconds for the two defense victories.** This is consistent with the fixed simulation timings and gives a comparison signal. It does not measure wall-clock play duration, time spent deciding/building, perceived waiting, or clarity of combat because this run had no rendered UI.

4. **The no-perk result still depends on the approach.** On generated seed 1 the economy policy wins without selecting rewards, while the defense policy loses at Night 5. The one-seed sample is not a balance acceptance gate.

## Strengths observed in the simulation

- The same documented purchase and perk preferences resolve deterministically by seed and produce night-by-night outcomes.
- Economy-first recovers from one blocked Farm location, and terrain variation did not change its victory in these three seeds.
- The reward path and income transaction complete through all five choices on winning normal runs, while the no-perk debug path is explicitly labeled in the log.
