## Brief and scope

The owner asked for an art-director pass toward a cohesive AA strategy look: a finished visual slice first (Keep, connected Walls, Archer Tower, one animated enemy, terrain, day/night lighting, combat effects), then the same style across the existing release content, plus the HUD. Balance, economy, navigation, saves, controls and campaign rules were not touched. No Core (simulation) file changed. Plan 009 content was not started.

## What changed

### Art direction (applied to everything below)

- A stylized medieval frontier. Weathered limestone, timber framing, iron fittings, red-brown shingle roofs, whitewashed plaster, muted vegetation, warm amber windows and torches.
- **Settlement** materials are warm. **Attackers** wear dark leather and iron, with their bible cloth colours (coral-brown, ochre, oxblood, olive, slate-violet, violet) as the identifying accent. Faint self-illumination keeps them readable at night. Colour semantics from the bible are unchanged: cyan is information, amber is player fire and economy, coral is threat.
- **Silhouette first.** Height bands from the art bible are kept. Every kind still has its bible tell, now modelled: Cannon is the crenellated pit with a barrel, Watchtower the stilts and pennant, Repair Station the crane, Raider Archer the bow, Bomber the keg on the back, Commander the banner and cape, Golem the stone mass with a glowing core.

### Visual slice

| Piece | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Keep | Battered plinth, string courses, four corner turrets with crenellated shingle caps, machicolated parapet, steep shingle roof with finial and settlement banner, arched studded door, two wall torches, slit windows. The window material glows with the night blend. 4.3k triangles |
| Connected Walls (6 shapes) | Sharpened-log palisade (7-sided logs, seeded lean and height) on a stone sill, an iron-capped joint post, and twin binding rails on both faces. Log spacing is derived so logs meet the neighbouring tile's logs at one spacing across the edge, so pieces join without gaps or overlaps. Height band 0.6–0.8 |
| Archer Tower | Battered stone footing, four leaning posts with X-bracing and a ring beam, overhanging plank platform, crenellated board parapet, ladder, shingle roof, torch with a night light. It has a **player archer** who turns to aim (same traverse code as the Cannon gun), draws between shots and snaps forward on each `TowerShot` |
| Grunt (animated) | Part rig: Hips > Torso > Head/ArmL/ArmR > Weapon; LegL/LegR. `CharacterRig` poses it from state WorldView already computes: stride from distance walked, weapon raised over the last 0.3 s before a due hit, swing down on contact, a flinch when the projectile **lands** (`shotFlight`) or a trap/burn ticks, and slack limbs during the fall. Everything runs on the presentation clock, so it freezes on pause, follows Night speed and never feeds back to the simulation |
| Terrain | Textured ground, soil banks and beach, stone cliff edge, scrolling water normals. Modelled pines, broadleaf trees and bushes on forest cells; boulder groups on rock cells; stepping stones on fords; rocks and reeds on the shore. Low tufts (under 0.08) on open land, **derived from the played map** outside a readability mask (reserved ring with entries and gates, the Keep and its neighbours, water banks, fords and both sides of each ford). Tufts hide while a building stands on their cell. Reeds and pebbles go only in water cells. Four hazed distant islands sit beyond the sea at the frame corners |
| Lighting | Trilight ambient instead of flat. Warm sun by day; cool moonlight at night with softer shadows, dimmed sky reflections and water darkened so it does not glow. Amber Keep light, per-tower torch lights and windows by night. Values live in `Art/ArtDirection.asset` |
| Post | Two global URP volumes cross-faded by the night amount: neutral tonemapping, mild contrast and saturation, bloom only above HDR white (flames, eyes, fuse), soft vignette. No depth of field, motion blur, grain or lens effects |
| Combat effects | Modelled arrows with a short pale trail. Stone sparks or timber splinters plus dust where an enemy's blow lands (`EnemyAttacked`). The existing shell, splash, blast, collapse and fire effects are kept. All particles are bounded by the existing pool (900) |

### Extension to the existing release content

House (timber-framed cottage, glowing windows), Farm (tilled furrows, crops, wattle fence, shed, hay), Spike Trap (pit frame and iron spikes, ≤0.15, no shadow), Cannon (static platform; gun parts keep the `Carriage/Barrel/Muzzle/Wheel/Shot` contract so it still traverses), Watchtower, Repair Station (with torch), the entry gate (log posts, skull, coral war banner that still pulses for a new side), and Runner, Brute, Raider Archer, Bomber (keg, with the `WickTip`/`TorchFlame` contract kept), Siege Commander and Siege Golem, all on the shared rig.

### Interface

One generated sliced sprite for every panel, button and card: rounded corners, a lit 1 px rim and a soft vertical shade. A thin amber rule along each panel's top edge is the one shared ornament. Light text gets a drop shadow. Unaffordable cards are dimmed but **opaque** (they used to drop to 50% alpha and let the board show through). Layout, sizes, hotkeys and costs are unchanged. The legacy built-in font is kept: no font was downloaded.

## Ownership and regeneration safety

- Authored assets live in `Assets/TheLastFortress/Art/`, separate from the generated `Prefabs/` and `Materials/`. `Editor/ArtSetup.cs` (**The Last Fortress > Art > Import and Assign Art Slice**) creates materials, post profiles and `ArtDirection.asset` **only when missing** and never rewrites them. Prefabs are rebuilt from the FBX models.
- `PrototypeSetup.Generated` now guards **every** blockout prefab write in the prototype, MVP and release setups. A content reference outside `Prefabs/<name>.prefab` is never overwritten. Before this, only `ReleaseSetup` had the guard; `PrototypeSetup` and `MvpSetup` overwrote the Keep, Wall, Tower, Grunt, House, Farm, trap, enemies and wall shapes on every run. `MvpSetup`/`ReleaseSetup` also carry the art direction down the chain.
- **Verified:** all three setups were run in the Editor after the art was assigned. Every authored reference survived in Prototype, MVP and Release content. The reruns rewrote unrelated generated files with line-ending and legacy-`_Color` noise only; those were restored from git.
- **Test (one, AGENTS.md):** `SerializedDataTests.AuthoredArtSurvivesBlockoutRegeneration` checks that release content points into `Art/`, and that the generator is not even invoked for those references. It fails if the guard is removed or the assignment is reverted.
- Provenance: [`Art/credits.md`](../game/The%20Last%20Fortress/Assets/TheLastFortress/Art/credits.md). Sources are in `Art/Source~/` (not imported by Unity).

| Build | median ms (3 runs) | mean of medians | p99 ms (3 runs) | gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (`Builds/Baseline`, HEAD 9d654b9) | 3.176 / 3.120 / 3.122 | **3.14** | 6.55 / 5.64 / 5.52 | pass ×3 |

The art pass costs about **+1.0 ms per frame (+31%)** in the 100-enemy fight, about 240 fps instead of 320. That is still far inside the 16.7 / 20 / 33.3 ms gate. Files: [`evidence/011-visual/perf-repeats/`](evidence/011-visual/perf-repeats/). The whole machine ran about 0.5 ms slower in this set than in an earlier identical set (baseline 2.60–2.71 ms then), which is why only paired, alternating runs are compared.

**Profile (Editor Play mode, stress fixture, `UnityStats`, before trimming):** batches 160 → 836, SetPass 47 → 257, triangles 318k → 560k. Enemies went from about 800 single-material primitives to 812 rig-part renderers with 1,932 submeshes, nearly all casting shadows. About 300 particles were live at once. Each particle is its own unbatched renderer.

**Trims applied (all presentation-only):**
1. Rig enemies cast shadows only from the torso and legs (`ArtSetup.AddRig`). Each enemy's shadow-casting submeshes drop from 20 to 8; the silhouette shadow on the ground stays.
2. The arrow trail is now a fixed streak inside the pooled arrow. Before, it emitted one particle per *rendered frame*, so its cost grew with frame rate (about 24 live particles per arrow at 250 fps).
3. Enemy strike debris drops from 6 to 3 particles per hit, and each building emits at most one burst per 0.2 s. A crowd hammering an invulnerable fixture Wall no longer floods the particle pool.
4. **Tried and reverted:** 2 shadow cascades with a mid-board split. The board's buildings fell into a coarse far cascade and their shadows nearly vanished. That is a visible loss, so the original 4 cascades / 50 m are kept.

**What the remaining +1.0 ms is:** roughly 0.3 ms of pixel shading and 0.7 ms of per-object CPU work from more renderers and submeshes (about 3–4 materials per building piece, rig parts, environment props). Further cuts would be structural: one material and texture atlas per building, or static batching of Walls. That's not justified at about 240 fps on this hardware. Revisit if a lower-end target appears.

**Build-path pitfall found:** players built in batchmode today rendered **without main-light shadows**, while the same project built from the open Editor shows them correctly (verified by a side-by-side capture of the same frame). The earlier batchmode baseline build did have shadows, so the cause is unexplained. Until it is understood, **build releases from the Editor** (`The Last Fortress > Build Release`) and check one capture for shadows. The final `Builds/Release` and every capture in `after/` come from an Editor build. The earlier locked-session A/B (2.52 → 4.06 ms) and the untrimmed 4.21 ms run used batchmode builds, so treat them as superseded.

Editor render stats for the baseline only: [file](evidence/011-visual/before/editor-render-stats.txt). The stress files say `scene=MVP (...)` for both builds; that is a pre-existing label in `StressMeasurement` (`IsMvp` is also true in the release scene).

## Updated presentation guidance (supersedes the blockout budgets where they conflict)

- **Triangles:** Keep about 4.3k, Archer Tower 3.2k, Wall piece about 1.1k, Grunt rig 1.0k. Larger enemies scale with the same parts.
- **LOD:** the bible asked for LOD1 on assets over about 2k triangles. That rule is **not applied** for now. Under a fixed orthographic camera every object is the same distance from the camera, and the zoom range is narrow, so LOD switching would mostly swap visible detail at default zoom without saving much. Revisit it if the visible-window measurement shows vertex cost matters.
- **Shared materials:** 51 shared URP Lit materials with 512 px tileable maps; everything is SRP-batcher compatible. The view animates runtime **copies** of the window, water and sea materials, never the assets.
- **Name contracts** the runtime relies on (keep them in any replacement): `Torch*` empties, Cannon `Carriage/Barrel/Muzzle/Wheel/Shot`, tower `Archer/Bow`, Bomber `WickTip/TorchFlame`, gate `Banner`, rig joints `Hips/Torso/Head/ArmL/ArmR/Weapon/LegL/LegR`. Models face +Z, pivots on the ground (footprint centre for 2×2), no colliders.
